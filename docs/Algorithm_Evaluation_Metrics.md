# Algorithm Evaluation Metrics for InvenSight: Precision, Recall, and F1 Measure

In demand forecasting and inventory management, standard regression metrics like Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), and Symmetric Mean Absolute Percentage Error (SMAPE) quantify forecasting deviation. However, they do not directly measure the **operational decision-making accuracy** of the system.

For a business, the critical decision is binary: **"Should we trigger a reorder alert for this product today?"** 

By framing demand forecasting as a **Binary Classification Problem** (Reorder Alert vs. No Alert), we can apply classification metrics—**Precision, Recall, and F1 Measure**—to evaluate how effectively the algorithm prevents stockouts while minimizing unnecessary capital lockup (overstocking).

---

## 1. Problem Formulation: Decision Mapping

Our forecasting engine (combining Singular Spectrum Analysis [SSA] and Facebook Prophet) predicts the 30-day demand ($D_{30}$) for each product. 
* Let $S_{current}$ be the current inventory stock level.
* Let $R$ be the reorder threshold (reorder level).

The system generates a **Reorder Alert** ($Y_{pred} = 1$) if the predicted 30-day demand exceeds the current stock, or if the stock falls below the safety reorder level:
$$Y_{pred} = \begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if } D_{30} > S_{current} \text{ or } S_{current} \le R \quad (\text{Reorder Alert}) \\ 
0 & \text{otherwise} \quad (\text{No Alert}) 
\end{cases}$$

To evaluate the model's performance historically, we compare this prediction with the **Actual Outcome** ($Y_{actual}$) over the subsequent 30-day period:
$$Y_{actual} = \begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if Actual 30-day Sales } > S_{current} \quad (\text{Actual Stockout / Reorder Needed}) \\ 
0 & \text{otherwise} \quad (\text{No Stockout occurred}) 
\end{cases}$$

---

## 2. The Confusion Matrix for Inventory Alerts

Comparing $Y_{pred}$ and $Y_{actual}$ gives us four possible outcomes:

| Metric | Prediction ($Y_{pred} = 1$) | Prediction ($Y_{pred} = 0$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Actual ($Y_{actual} = 1$)** | **True Positive (TP)**<br>System predicted a stockout/reorder, and the product indeed ran out of stock. | **False Negative (FN)**<br>System predicted no stockout, but the product ran out (Missed Alert / Stockout!). |
| **Actual ($Y_{actual} = 0$)** | **False Positive (FP)**<br>System predicted a stockout/reorder, but the product did not run out (False Alarm / Overstocking risk). | **True Negative (TN)**<br>System predicted no stockout, and the inventory remained sufficient. |

### Operational Meanings:
* **True Positive (TP)**: Efficient inventory replenishment. Capital is used correctly to prevent a stockout.
* **False Positive (FP)**: Overstocking. The system recommended buying more stock, but the demand wasn't there, leading to high holding costs and tied-up capital.
* **False Negative (FN)**: Understocking/Stockout. The system failed to warn the admin, resulting in lost sales and customer dissatisfaction.
* **True Negative (TN)**: Ideal operational state. No unnecessary capital spent, and no stockout occurred.

---

## 3. Mathematical Formulas & Interpretations

### A. Precision (Positive Predictive Value)
Precision measures the accuracy of the reorder recommendations. It answers: *“Of all the reorder alerts generated by InvenSight, how many were actually necessary?”*

$$\text{Precision} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FP}}$$

* **Low Precision:** Means the system generates frequent false alarms, leading to **overstocking** and excess storage costs.
* **High Precision:** Proves that when the algorithm recommends purchasing, the business can trust that the items will sell.

### B. Recall (Sensitivity / True Positive Rate)
Recall measures the system's coverage of stockout events. It answers: *“Of all the products that actually ran out of stock, how many did InvenSight successfully warn us about in advance?”*

$$\text{Recall} = \frac{\text{TP}}{\text{TP} + \text{FN}}$$

* **Low Recall:** Means the algorithm is missing critical stockout events, leading to **understocking** and lost revenue.
* **High Recall:** Proves that the algorithm is highly sensitive and effectively protects the store from ever running dry.

### C. F1 Measure (F1-Score)
Precision and Recall are often in tension. If you alert on everything (high Recall), Precision drops (lots of False Positives). If you only alert when 100% sure (high Precision), Recall drops (lots of missed stockouts). 

The F1 Measure is the **harmonic mean** of Precision and Recall, providing a single metric of overall system accuracy:

$$\text{F1 Measure} = 2 \times \frac{\text{Precision} \times \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}} = \frac{2\text{TP}}{2\text{TP} + \text{FP} + \text{FN}}$$

* **F1-Score of 1.0 (100%):** Perfect predictions (no stockouts and no overstocking).
* **High F1-Score:** Proves that the model achieves the optimal balance between holding costs (FP) and stockout costs (FN).
